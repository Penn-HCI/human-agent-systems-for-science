# Creative writing with a machine in the loop (Clark et al., 2018)

## Summary
A user study of writing stories and slogans with or without AI suggestions. Answer: mostly don't correlate. Writers liked writing with the AI even when its suggestions and their outputs were rated poorly.

## Relevant findings
- **Writers' ratings of their own work barely correlate with crowdworker ratings of it (r = 0.04–0.13 for creativity and liking).**

> [!quote]- Source: Clark et al. 2018, p. 8
> "The average Turker score and the self-evaluation score had Pearson correlation coefficients of 0.08, 0.07, and 0.04 for how creative the story was, how entertaining it was, and how much they liked it, respectively. The correlations for coherence and grammaticality were slightly better (0.31 and 0.41, respectively) but still weak."
>
> [[sources/clark-2018-creative-writing-machine-loop-case.pdf]] · https://doi.org/10.1145/3172944.3172983

- **Slogan writers with AI were more satisfied (5.22 vs. 4.07), even though they rated the suggestions low and crowdworkers rated their slogans less creative (3.84 vs. 4.37).**

> [!quote]- Source: Clark et al. 2018, p. 8
> "Participant self-evaluation of the final slogans was generally higher in the MIL condition than the solo condition, even though the scores for the slogan suggestions were low. For example, the mean score for final slogans from the MIL condition was higher than in the solo case for both satisfaction (5.22 vs. 4.07, p<0.01) and creativity (4.48 vs. 3.93, p=0.15)."
>
> [[sources/clark-2018-creative-writing-machine-loop-case.pdf]] · https://doi.org/10.1145/3172944.3172983

> [!quote]- Source: Clark et al. 2018, p. 9
> "The Amazon Mechanical Turk third-party evaluations rated the slogans written in the MIL condition as slightly less creative than the slogans from the solo condition, giving an average score of 3.84 and 4.37 (p=0.03), respectively."
>
> [[sources/clark-2018-creative-writing-machine-loop-case.pdf]] · https://doi.org/10.1145/3172944.3172983
